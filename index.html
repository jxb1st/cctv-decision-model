CCTV decision model — typed anomaly decisions from frames (Laya-Vision levels 1–3 vs Qwen3.8-27B)

2026-10-14 in-progress CCTVanomaly detectiondecision modelLaya-VisionETRI

First pass at the mentor's decision-model proposal on the ETRI CCTV dataset: calibrated typed anomaly decisions (noul + choice) from frames with Laya-Vision (201M), zero-shot on single frames (Level 1) and 4-frame windows (Level 2), fine-tuned on the ETRI temporal ground truth (Level 3), against Qwen3.8-27B answering the same typed questions per frame. Two of five classes so far (abandoned_object, falling; 125 videos). Zero-shot Laya-Vision is at chance (frame AUC 0.51–0.59); 30 minutes of fine-tuning on one MIG slice brings the 1-frame model to AUC 0.73 on 41 test videos, level with Qwen3.8-27B (0.71 on its 12 videos) and ahead of it in event recall at a 1 % false-positive budget (39 % vs 25 %), at 150 ms per decision.

Overview

The proposal replaces "a large video VLM describes the scene" with a small decision model: the model is given a state (one or several frames) and typed questions, and returns calibrated probabilities in one forward pass, without generating text. This experiment runs the first three levels of that proposal on the ETRI CCTV dataset and compares them with a large VLM asked the same typed questions.

  • Level 1: Laya-Vision (SmolVLM-256M backbone + decision head, 201M parameters) on single frames, zero-shot.
  • Level 2: the same model on a window of the last 4 frames (4 s of context), zero-shot.
  • Level 3: Laya-Vision fine-tuned on the ETRI train split with the repository's RLCD objective, single-frame and 4-frame variants.
  • Reference: Qwen3.8-27B answering the same questions as lettered choices; the distribution is the softmax over the letter logits (the vidjev protocol).

No footage is shown on this page: the ETRI videos are not public. Score curves, metrics and prompts only.

Research Question

Can a 200M-parameter typed decision model, run on every frame of a CCTV stream, separate annotated abnormal events from normal footage, and how far is it from a 27B VLM on the same questions? Does a short temporal window help, and does fine-tuning on the ETRI temporal ground truth close the gap?

Experimental Setup

Data
ETRI_CCTV_dataset_20261008, classes available at build time: abandoned_object, falling. 125 videos (84 train / 41 test; test = video number divisible by 3), 12.9 h of 1080p30 footage, one annotated event per video (XML: type, subtype, start mm:ss, duration s) plus scene tags. Frames sampled at 1 fps, 640×360 JPEG, the same frames for every model.
Label
Frame label = 1 inside the XML event span, else 0. For abandoned objects only the 20 s act is annotated; the object left behind afterwards counts as normal.
Models
Laya-Vision thaitea/laya-vision @ f2fe3c1 (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 weights); Qwen3.8-27B @ 1d4bf0f, BF16, non-thinking mode.
Questions
Two typed questions per decision: anomaly (noul: P(yes)) and event (6-way choice: normal + 5 classes). Anomaly score = the noul; secondary score = 1 − P(normal).
Metrics
Frame-level AUC and AP over all scored test frames; per-class AUC inside that class's videos; ECE (15 bins); event recall / median delay / false-alarm onsets per hour at thresholds 0.5, 0.7, 0.9; wall-clock ms per decision.
Hardware
Laya-Vision: one H200 MIG slice (1g.18gb), torch 2.6 cu124, transformers 5.17. Qwen3.8-27B: one full H200, transformers 5.17 (reference PyTorch kernels for the gated delta-net layers).

Method / Pipeline

  1. Index videos + XML; split by video number
  2. Sample 1 fps frames (640×360)
  3. Per frame (or last-4-frame window): typed questions → P(anomaly), P(event type)
  4. Fine-tune Laya-Vision on train frames (balanced pos/neg, RLCD loss), refit temperatures on held-out train videos
  5. Score test frames; frame AUC, event metrics, calibration

Window decisions are attributed to the latest frame. Fine-tuning trains the decision head and the last 4 language-model layers; each training frame contributes one noul example (anomaly label) and one choice example (class inside the span, normal outside).

Observations

  • Zero-shot Laya-Vision is a coin flip. Frame AUC 0.51 (1 frame) / 0.57 (4 frames) on the 4,385 common frames and 0.59 on all 41 test videos; P(anomaly) wanders between 0.1 and 0.6 whatever is in the frame. This matches the model card: the released checkpoint is a base for fine-tuning, not a zero-shot detector.
  • The 4-frame window does not help zero-shot and costs 3.5× the time (527 vs 150 ms per decision on a MIG slice): the scores only get smoother and lower. The fine-tuned 4-frame variant is still running.
  • Fine-tuning is what makes the model. 80 train videos (23,550 frames, balanced sampling, 3,300 steps, ~30 min on 1/7 of an H200, decision head + last 4 LM layers) lift the 1-frame model to AUC 0.73 on all 41 test videos (0.69 on the common frames), next to Qwen3.8-27B's 0.71, with a model 135× smaller that answers in 150 ms on a MIG slice (Qwen: 220 ms on a full H200).
  • At a 1 % frame false-positive budget the fine-tuned model finds more events than Qwen: 39 % of the 41 test events (42 % of the 12 common ones) vs 25 %; at 5 % it is 68 % vs 33 %. Qwen is precise when it fires (its score is below 0.03 on half the frames and its best 1 % operating point gives 1.7 alarms/h) but it misses most events; the fine-tuned Laya trades alarms for recall (18.6 alarms/h at the same point).
  • Falling is the class the models see; abandoned objects are not. Falling: Qwen 0.84, fine-tuned Laya 0.76–0.81; abandoned_object stays at 0.54–0.62 for every run. The act (putting down a bag and walking off) is a few pixels in a 640×360 frame, and the GT labels only the 20 s act: the object left behind counts as normal, so any detector that reacts to the object is penalised.
  • The fine-tuned noul is badly calibrated (ECE 0.35). The refit temperature hit the grid maximum (10), squeezing every score into 0.26–0.67, and the calibration frames (60 negatives per held-out video) are 35 % positive against 7 % on the test stream. Qwen's letter-logit scores are better calibrated (ECE 0.09); the zero-shot 4-frame run's ECE of 0.05 only reflects that it predicts a low score almost always.
  • Fine-tuning drifted after step ~600. Held-out accuracy peaked at 0.62–0.64 (steps 300–600) and ended at 0.57 (step 3,300); the saved model is the last step. Best-checkpoint selection and a shorter schedule are the obvious fixes before the five-class rerun.
  • Event type (choice head). Qwen answers normal on 99.3 % of frames outside the spans, but inside the spans its choice matches the video's class only 18 % of the time (it says normal there too); the fine-tuned Laya matches 49 % (35 % on common frames) and still says normal on 89 % of the normal frames.

Conclusion

On the two classes available so far the decision-model route works as a mechanism: a 201M typed model fine-tuned for half an hour on one MIG slice reaches the frame AUC of a 27B VLM asked the same questions (0.73 vs 0.71), beats it in event recall at a fixed false-positive budget, and decides in 150 ms per frame. Zero-shot it is useless, and 4 s of temporal context has not helped yet. The absolute numbers are modest (AUC 0.73, abandoned_object at chance, calibration broken by the temperature refit), two of five classes are covered, and Qwen was scored on 12 of the 41 test videos, so this is a first pass that says the route is worth continuing, not a result.

Next Steps

  • Add fight, intrusion and loitering as soon as the archives arrive (Google Drive quota at the moment of writing) and rerun every level; scoring is resumable, the fine-tunes are redone on all five classes.
  • Fine-tuning: keep the best held-out checkpoint instead of the last step, calibrate on natural-prevalence frames of the held-out videos, shorter schedule; finish the fine-tuned 4-frame variant (running).
  • Level 2 properly: longer and sparser windows (e.g. 8 frames at 0.5 fps) and frame differences, the cases where loitering and abandoned objects need context.
  • A second reading of the abandoned_object GT (object left behind = positive) to measure how much of that class's gap is annotation rather than perception.
  • Two-stage observe → decide: Qwen3.8-27B description text into the text Laya decision model, the jev-vlm-driver-anomaly recipe.
